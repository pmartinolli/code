Scholarly Timelined Network: a Genealogy of Written Works by Backward Citations

List of works (table view)
About this tool and its parameters

This tool draws the genealogy of a written work on a timeline: the works it relies on, the works those rely on, and so on, going backward only. The data comes from Wikidata and is queried live, from your browser, on the QLever endpoint (which holds the complete graph, whereas the Wikidata Query Service has been split between scholarly articles and everything else since 2024). By default it starts from « Zur Theorie der Gesellschaftsspiele » by Von Neumann, 1928 (Q55867220), a seminal paper of game theory.

Parameters

Start
The Wikidata identifier (QID) of the starting work; a Wikidata address can be pasted as well. If the item is an edition or a translation, the genealogy starts from its generic work.
Links
The statements followed from a work to its sources. « cites work » (P2860) and « acknowledged » (P7137) are the stronger links, drawn as solid lines; « inspired by » (P941) and « based on » (P144) are drawn as dotted lines. Untick a kind of link to leave it out.
Depth
The number of generations followed backward (1 to 10): 1 shows only the works cited by the starting work, 2 adds the works these cite, etc.
Max
The maximum number of works displayed (10 to 3000). When a network is larger, the most cited works of each generation are kept first and the status line tells how many were left out.
Scale
« Linear » gives every year the same width. « Compressed past » keeps room for the recent decades and progressively tightens the centuries and millennia before them. « Auto » compresses the past when the network spans more than 400 years.

What is retrieved

  • Works only. People, institutions and other non-works are ignored, even when a statement points to them (« acknowledged » mostly points to people).
  • Editions grouped. Versions, editions and translations (P629 / P747) are merged with their generic work, as citing and as cited items. An edition gathering several works stays on its own.
  • Dates. The earliest of inception (P571) and publication date (P577) is used, with its precision: a day, a year, a decade, a century or a millennium, BCE included. An imprecise date is drawn as a pale bar covering its period. Without any date, the work is placed from the active years of its author or from its earliest edition (dashed outline), or else just before the works citing it (hollow dot).

Reading and moving

  • The starting work is orange; the blue gets lighter with each generation. The size of a dot grows with the number of times the work is cited in the network.
  • Hover a work to see its details and highlight its links; click to open it in Wikidata; Alt+click to restart from it.
  • Scroll moves vertically, Ctrl+scroll zooms the timeline, Shift+scroll spreads or tightens the rows, drag moves the view, double-click zooms in on a date.
  • The address of the page keeps the parameters, so a network can be shared as a link.

Limits

Work in progress. It is better suited to older works, whose citations were indexed by hand, than to recent works with numerous and dense citation links. QLever only accepts about one request per second after a short burst, so large networks take a few more seconds.
